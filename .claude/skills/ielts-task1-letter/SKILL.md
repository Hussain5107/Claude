# IELTS Writing Task 1 Letter

Source: notes transcribed from an e2language.com IELTS prep video on Task 1 formal letters.

## What the examiner scores (4 criteria)

1. **Topic** — Answer every bullet point in the prompt, one bullet per paragraph. Never blend paragraphs together.
2. **Precise vocabulary** — Only use words/phrases/collocations you're 100% sure of. A simple, correct word beats an impressive, wrong one.
3. **Accurate grammar** — Correct verb tense, articles (the/a/none), singular vs plural nouns, prepositions. Mix short simple sentences with longer complex ones (joined with because/that/which/where). If unsure of grammar, prefer short and clear over long and unclear.
4. **Logical structure** — Follow the 5-part structure below exactly.

Formal, semi-formal, and informal letters use ~98% the same structure and language; only a small slice of vocabulary/tone changes (e.g. "Dear Sir or Madam" vs a first name, and how polite the requests sound).

## The 5-part structure

Roughly 25% of every letter is fixed boilerplate that can be memorized word-for-word; the rest is filled in from the specific prompt.

1. **Greeting** (memorize)
   - Formal, name unknown: `Dear Sir or Madam,`
   - Name known: `Dear [Title] [Surname],`

2. **Paragraph 1 — Reason for writing** (general, not detailed — save details for paragraph 2)
   - Opening phrase: `I am writing to let you know that / to inform you that...`
   - Covers the first bullet point only (describe the situation).

3. **Paragraph 2 — Explanation / details**
   - Opening phrase: `Let me explain my situation...` / `Let me explain the problem in more detail...`
   - Covers the second bullet point (the specific problems and why they matter/cause difficulty).

4. **Paragraph 3 — Request or suggestion**
   - Opening phrase for a request: `Would you kindly [verb]...`
   - Covers the third bullet point (what the writer wants done).

5. **Sign-off** (memorize)
   - `Thank you for your consideration.`
   - `Yours faithfully,` (name unknown) or `Yours sincerely,` (name known)
   - `[Name]`

## Worked example (Band 9 model, 156 words)

Prompt: *You live in a room in college which you share with another student. There are problems with this arrangement and you find it difficult to work. Write a letter to the accommodation officer. Describe the situation, explain your problems and why it is difficult to work, say what kind of accommodation you would prefer.*

> Dear Sir or Madam,
>
> I'm writing to let you know that I am unhappy with my current living conditions. While the accommodation itself is fine, my housemate has become intolerable, making it almost impossible for me to work. I hope you will consider my situation favorably.
>
> Let me explain my situation. The trouble started about three months ago when my housemate first moved in; prior to that, everything was fine. My old housemate was extremely considerate. My current housemate, however, stays up almost all night and plays incessantly loud music. As such, I have not been able to sleep properly and therefore cannot concentrate on my studies.
>
> Would you kindly transfer me or my housemate to another room? I would much prefer to live with someone who is quieter and more considerate of others; as long as it is quiet, I don't mind whom I live with.
>
> Thank you for your consideration.
>
> Yours faithfully,
> Jay

Notable vocabulary/collocations used: *unhappy with, current living conditions, intolerable, almost impossible, consider ... favorably, extremely considerate, plays incessantly loud music, concentrate on, transfer, more considerate of.*

## How to use this skill

When given an IELTS Task 1 letter prompt:
1. Identify the 3 bullet points and map them to paragraphs 2–4 above (1 = greeting is separate).
2. Draft ~150 words: short general paragraph 1, detailed paragraph 2, clear request/suggestion paragraph 3.
3. Check the draft against the 4 scoring criteria before presenting it.
4. If asked to *review* a user's letter rather than write one, score it against the 4 criteria and point out specifically where paragraphs blend, where vocabulary is imprecise, or where grammar (articles/tense/prepositions) is off.
